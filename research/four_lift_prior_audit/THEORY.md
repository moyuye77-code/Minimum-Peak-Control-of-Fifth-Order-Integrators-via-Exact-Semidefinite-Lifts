# Exact attribution audit of the joint four-moment representation

2026-09-28. This is an algebraic audit, not a second main contribution or a
completed priority search. The candidate K4 theorem remains mathematically
unchanged. The audit separates a classical moment condition, established
lifting methodology, and the still-unresolved priority of this application.

## 1. Exact correspondence to the exponential transform

Write y=(m,z,q,r), the integrals of (1,t,t^2,t^3) against a density in [0,1]
on [0,1]. Expand the classical exponential transform as a formal series:

    1-exp[-(m w+z w^2+q w^3+r w^4+...)] = sum_(j>=0) a_j w^(j+1).

The first four coefficients, generated by coefficient extraction, are

    a0=m,
    a1=z-m^2/2,
    a2=q-mz+m^3/6,
    a3=r-mq-z^2/2+m^2 z/2-m^4/24.

Let H=[[a1,a2],[a2,a3]] and C=[[1,0],[m/2,1]]. Direct multiplication gives

    C H C^T = G(y)
            = [[z-m^2/2, q-mz/2-m^3/12],
               [q-mz/2-m^3/12, r-z^2/2-m^2 z/4]].           (A)

Since det(C)=1, the equivalence H>=0 iff G>=0 holds also on singular faces;
it does not divide by z-m^2/2. Thus the nonlinear matrix G used in the
candidate is a coordinate-dependent congruence of this classical matrix,
not an independent new bounded-density moment inequality.

The same holds for ybar=(1-m,1/2-z,1/3-q,1/4-r). The fact that the pair
G(y),G(ybar)>=0 is sufficient on the finite interval is supplied by our
previous boundary construction in moment_upper_gate/THEORY.md and
moment_four_lift/THEORY.md, NOT by silently treating a half-line necessary
condition as a complete finite-interval theorem.

Source: Tkachev, *Sharp pointwise gradient estimates for Riesz potentials
with a bounded density*, https://d-nb.info/1162062126/34, Section 1,
equation (10) and adjacent discussion. Those passages credit the classical
Markov transform. We use that transform, not the questionable printed
exponent in equation (11). The four-coefficient expansion and congruence
above were performed here. No claim is made to have read the cited
Ahiezer--Krein book in full.

## 2. Derivation of the certificate by Hessian integration

Use coordinate variables (s,t) for (m,z), and fix a vector xi=(alpha,beta).
The variables q,r enter G affinely. Define phi=-xi^T G xi and D=t-s^2/2.
Differentiation with respect to (s,t) gives the elementary factorization

    Hessian(phi) = vv^T + (beta^2 D/2) diag(1,0),
    v = (alpha+beta*s/2, beta)^T.                            (B)

Consequently G is matrix-concave on D>=0. This is domain-restricted:
for beta=1, alpha=0, s=0, t=-1, the Hessian's first diagonal entry is -1/2.
It is NOT globally SOS-concave.

Let (S,T) be the reference coordinates, u=s-S, v0=t-T and D0=T-S^2/2.
Along the straight segment, an exact identity is

    D(S+tau*u,T+tau*v0)
       = (1-tau)D0+tau D+tau(1-tau)u^2/2.

Integrate the Hessian Taylor remainder with weight (1-tau), tau in [0,1].
Substituting (B), integrating its polynomial entries, and completing a
square gives

    phi(s,t)-phi(S,T)-grad(phi)(S,T) dot (u,v0)
      = 1/2 [beta(v0+S*u/2+u^2/6)+alpha*u]^2
         + beta^2 [D*u^2/12+D0*u^2/6+u^4/36].               (C)

This is an identity in every variable, including beta=0, and certifies
nonnegativity when D,D0>=0. In that zero-beta case it becomes alpha^2 u^2/2.
With xi=(-g/2,1), (C) recovers the previous scalar-parametric certificate.

**Attribution:** Hessian integration, positive-functional Jensen arguments,
and localizing-matrix lifts are established methods. Lasserre,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/0806.3784, Lemmas 2.4--2.5 and Theorem 2.6 with its
proof were read this turn. Theorem 2.6 assumes global SOS-convexity, so (B)
does not license a verbatim invocation of that theorem. Nie,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/0908.0364, Section 4, definition (4.2), the lifting
formulas and Lemma 4.1/Theorem 4.2/Corollary 4.3 with proofs were inspected.
The domain-weighted pattern in (C) fits that established methodology.
Its displayed theorem also assumes S subset int(D); our prefix density
1_[0,1/2] lies on D=0. The existing direct positive-functional proof handles
this boundary. Failure of a quoted theorem's hypothesis is neither a proof
that the application is novel nor grounds to omit the boundary argument.

In particular, (C) should not be advertised as a separate new Jensen theorem
or a separate main contribution. Its specific derivation makes the K4
application checkable.

## 3. Automatic standard-coordinate moment construction

Take all monomials of total degree at most four in s,t. Let Lambda(1)=1,
Lambda(s)=m, Lambda(t)=z, with twelve other coordinate moments free. q,r
remain original scalar decision variables; they are not additional moment
coordinates. Define

    V2=(1,s,t,s^2,st,t^2), V1=(1,s,t),
    M2=Lambda(V2 V2^T),
    Nminus=Lambda((t-s^2/2)V1 V1^T),
    Nplus=Lambda((s-t-s^2/2)V1 V1^T).

Impose M2,Nminus,Nplus,Lambda(G),Lambda(Gbar)>=0. This is a generic
order-two coordinate-moment construction with block sizes 6,3,3,2,2 and
twelve auxiliaries. It is NOT the ordinary higher-time-moment domination
hierarchy previously shown non-exact. Here the additional variables are
moments of polynomials in the coordinates m,z.

The generator takes the exponential coefficients, forms C H C^T, constructs
the Gram/localizing polynomials, then coefficient-linearizes them. It does
not call an optimizer or paste the candidate's seven matrix formulas to
generate the standard model. The existing formulas are imported only for
the final comparison.

Set

    a=Lambda(s^2), b=Lambda(s^3), c=Lambda(s^4),
    d=Lambda(st), e=Lambda(s^2 t), f=Lambda(t^2).

All seven candidate matrices are now obtained as follows:

| Candidate block | Exact standard construction |
|---|---|
| H | restrict M2 to (1,s,s^2) |
| Jplus | restrict M2 to (1,s,t+s^2/6) |
| Jminus | restrict M2 to (1,s,t-s^2/6) |
| Tminus | restrict Nminus to (1,s) |
| Tplus | restrict Nplus to (1,s) |
| Gminus | Lambda(G) |
| Gplus | Lambda(Gbar) |

Every entry agrees exactly; there are 43 matrix entries in this comparison,
including symmetric duplicates. Six moments present in the generic model
are absent in the sparse construction: Lambda(st^2), Lambda(t^3),
Lambda(s^3t), Lambda(s^2t^2), Lambda(st^3), Lambda(t^4).

The two projected feasible sets are exactly the same K4. In one direction,
the displayed restrictions map any standard feasible point to a candidate
feasible point, which projects into K4 by the existing direct proof. In the
other direction, for any y in K4 take Lambda(s^i t^j)=m^i z^j. M2 is rank
one; the localizers are nonnegative multiples of rank-one matrices; the
last two blocks equal G(y),G(ybar). This is a feasible standard lift.

This is equality of projections, not a statement that every sparse
auxiliary tuple admits completion to a standard moment matrix. We have not
proved that stronger auxiliary-completion statement and do not need it.

## 4. What this audit decides

1. The nonlinear four-moment inequalities and the generic lifting method
   are not new. The certificate is a domain-weighted Hessian/Jensen
   specialization and should be presented with those antecedents.
2. The seven-block system is an exact sparse version of the standard
   coordinate lift for this particular congruent PMI. The six-variable
   saving is not an independently sufficient journal contribution.
3. This audit has not located a published statement proving this same K4
   affine representability result. A derivation with established methods
   does not demonstrate prior publication of its conclusion. Conversely,
   a search non-hit does not establish priority. The specific K4 theorem
   remains a candidate, not cleared novelty and not rejected merely for
   using established tools.
4. A defensible single paper must rest on the concrete reach-set
   representability/extension-complexity result and its importance, rather
   than counting the transform, certificate, compression, observer, and
   verifier as five innovations. The independent high-order SOC obstruction
   belongs to this same structural question; it is not an automatic second
   paper.
5. Closed-loop superiority cannot follow just from another exact
   representation of the same information set. Equivalent exact methods
   should yield the same mathematical decisions. Runtime, reliability and
   composition with a control synthesis problem need separate evidence.
   Lack of runtime advantage does not refute a representation theorem.

No new numerical solves, timing experiments, closed loops or external
coordination were performed by this module. This does not complete the
requested paper, prove general-order SDP representability, settle the K4
SOC question, or prove any acceptance probability.
