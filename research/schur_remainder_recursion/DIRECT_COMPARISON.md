## Claim being audited

The bounded-density body K5 in its original five integral-moment coordinates
has a finite affine PSD lift, including its singular boundary, but no lift over
any finite product of Lorentz cones. The constructive half uses the weighted
L5 remainder and a separate shadow-closure operation; the obstruction is the
previous lower bound based on polynomial extension degree.

The general H_n reduction is supporting methodology, not a proved all-order
existence theorem. The open epigraph's block dimensions do not bound the final
closed lift by assertion. Numerical performance is not part of the new theorem.

Follow-up on the same date: the separate sparse-space proof in
`../schur_sparse_lift/THEORY.md` and its explicitly block-preserving closure
lemma strengthen the internally proved claim to 3<=sxdeg(K5)<=4. This does
not infer a closed model's auxiliary count from the open model, does not
settle minimality between three and four, and is not separately counted as
another innovation. The source-comparison scope below is unchanged.

## Search scope and what it does not establish

Queries included integrator reach sets with finite SDP/fifth order; Markov or
bounded-density moment bodies with spectrahedral/SOC lifts; and polynomial
L1 norms with semidefinite representability. The last query is relevant because
the centered body's support is one half the integral absolute polynomial.

Results in these passes did not locate an explicitly matching K5 separation.
Unrelated density-matrix moment bodies assume an SDP representation from the
outset and cannot establish it for this different body. Asymptotic bounded-
density hierarchies likewise require a finite-exactness argument to subsume
this claim. These distinctions are screening criteria, not proof that no older
general result can be specialized to K5.

Search-engine crawl dates were not used as publication dates. Earlier project
source audits remain necessary; this file is not a replacement for them.

## Earlier provisional adjudication (superseded by the latest link above)

Retain the category-separation theorem as a single candidate. It is stronger in
kind than reporting a smaller fourth-order formulation, but importance and
priority are still unconfirmed. Do not add closure, recovery, communication
scheduling and simulations as separate supposed innovations around it.

There are two independent remaining questions:

1. Is this specific result already a direct specialization of a published
   theorem, with all original-coordinate, bounded-density and boundary
   hypotheses actually matched?
2. If not, does it provide sufficient theoretical or control-computational
   value to support a coherent field-recognized paper?

An unfavorable answer to the second is not evidence that the theorem is false
or already published. Conversely, failure to find a match does not settle the
first. No journal acceptance probability or paper count is inferred here.

## Additional terminology screen, September 28

The new search pass included polynomial integral L1 norm, Markov/bounded-density
moment bodies, and zonoid/spectrahedral-shadow terms. It did not establish
an exhaustive absence of an equivalent theorem. In particular, coefficient
ell-1 norms, ordinary unrestricted-measure moment cones, and arbitrary convex
hulls of curves are not interchangeable with this bounded-density body.

The primary article [Spectral Polyhedra, Section 5, Spectral zonotopes and
zonoids](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/spectral-polyhedra/941BEFF902EB29C144B96DF8A97F2394)
was inspected as a terminology-level possible comparator. Its displayed
support constructions depend on matrix eigenvalues and permutation symmetry;
the inspected passage is not a theorem that every polynomial-curve zonoid has
an exact finite lift. No reduction of our K5 to that special construction was
established. This scoped exclusion does not exclude other results elsewhere
in that article or in the literature, and does not change the priority verdict.

The original [bounded-density moment paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607463)
remains a necessary comparator: its abstract describes conditions on full
moment sequences and finite checks. Finite checking alone is not a proof of
finite exactness for the five specified moments. Earlier full source and
truncation audits, rather than this abstract-level screen, support that
distinction. The general method attribution in the manuscript is retained.
